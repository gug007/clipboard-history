The best free clipboard managers for Mac.

Start with Apple's built-in option if you use macOS Tahoe. Choose Clipboard History or Maccy for a focused standalone app, or Raycast if you also want a full command launcher.

There is no universal winner. The right choice depends on your macOS version, the formats you copy, whether you want organization, and whether clipboard history should sync.

How this was compared: feature descriptions and cost models were checked against each developer's official pages on July 27, 2026. This is a use-case comparison, not a synthetic speed or memory benchmark. Features and plans can change.

1. macOS Tahoe Spotlight: best for occasional use

macOS Tahoe 26 includes searchable clipboard history in Spotlight. Press Command + Space, then Command + 4. You can search, copy an earlier item, and clear the history without installing another app.

It is the sensible first choice if you only lose a recent copy now and then. It is included with the operating system and maintained by Apple. The limitation is compatibility: Sequoia 15 and Sonoma 14 do not have this Spotlight history. Dedicated apps also offer different shortcuts and organization models.

Read Apple's Spotlight clipboard instructions or follow our version-by-version Mac guide.

2. Clipboard History: best for files, folders, and groups

Clipboard History is the app made by this site, so treat this section as first-party information. It is free and open source under the MIT License. It runs on macOS 14 or later and opens directly from any app with Shift + Command + V.

Its useful distinction is handling Finder files and folders by reference. Search their names, paste them again as files, or reveal the original with Command + R. Favorites and groups keep reusable clips outside normal automatic cleanup. History is local-only, and copies made while a supported password manager is frontmost are excluded.

It also preserves RTF or HTML with a plain-text fallback and stores direct PNG or TIFF clipboard images with bounded previews. The limits matter: there is no cloud sync, OCR, QR recognition, image-content search, or clip editor. See the format guide, security model, or inspect the source on GitHub.

3. Maccy: best for deliberate simplicity

Maccy describes itself as a clipboard manager that does one job: keep copy history at hand. Its official site emphasizes a lightweight, keyboard-first interface, open-source code, and password-manager-aware privacy. It requires macOS Sonoma 14 or later.

Choose Maccy if you want a mature, focused utility and do not need a broader productivity platform. Its narrow scope is a benefit for minimalists, but it may not match someone looking for a full launcher or the particular file-and-group workflow offered by Clipboard History.

Review Maccy's current features and download options, then see the detailed Maccy vs Clipboard History comparison.

4. Raycast: best if you want a command launcher too

Raycast combines clipboard history with app launching, commands, extensions, window management, and other productivity tools. Its official clipboard page lists text, images, colors, and links, plus search and pinned entries. Raycast says copied items are stored locally and encrypted.

Choose it if you already use Raycast or want its wider platform. If you only want a compact clipboard utility, installing and configuring a full launcher can be more than you need. Raycast has free and paid plans, so check whether the current retention and features on its free plan fit your workflow.

Review Raycast Clipboard History and its current official plan details before choosing. Our Raycast vs Clipboard History comparison goes deeper on the suite-versus-standalone decision.

Paid alternatives worth considering

Paste: best for history across Apple devices

Paste is a commercial clipboard manager for Mac, iPhone, and iPad. Its official site emphasizes searchable history, organization, and sync through private iCloud. Choose it when cross-device access matters more than a free, local-only setup. Check Paste's current features and pricing, then read Paste vs Clipboard History.

Alfred Powerpack: best for Alfred automation users

Alfred's clipboard history is part of its paid Powerpack. It handles text, images, and file links, and connects clipboard entries with Alfred's snippets and workflows. It is a natural choice if Alfred already anchors your Mac workflow; it is not a free clipboard-history option. Read Alfred's official Clipboard History guide and our Alfred vs Clipboard History comparison.

How to choose safely

  1. Check formats. Distinguish direct clipboard images, image files, rich text, OCR, and editing—“image support” can mean different things.
  2. Choose local or synced. Local-only history reduces cloud exposure; sync is more convenient across devices.
  3. Review exclusions. Look for concealed-data handling, password-manager exclusions, and an app skip list.
  4. Test retrieval. The best shortcut and search interface is the one you can use without interrupting your work.
  5. Know how to clear it. Clipboard history should be easy to pause, selectively delete, and erase completely.
