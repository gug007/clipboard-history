How to view clipboard history on Mac.

The right clipboard-history shortcut depends on your macOS version. Tahoe 26 includes searchable history in Spotlight; Sequoia and Sonoma need an app for multi-item history.

Tahoe instructionsSonoma & SequoiaUpdated July 28, 2026

The quick answer

macOS Tahoe 26: press Command + Space to open Spotlight, then press Command + 4 to open Apple's Clipboard view. Search for an item, copy it, and paste it where you need it.

macOS Sequoia 15 or Sonoma 14: macOS keeps your current copied item but has no searchable multi-item clipboard-history interface. Use a clipboard manager to retrieve earlier copies.

Clipboard History app on macOS 14 or later: press Shift + Command + V from any app to open your searchable history directly. See the complete Clipboard History app shortcut reference or download Clipboard History for Mac.

Apple documents those steps in its official guide to searching Clipboard history in Spotlight. The first time you open it, Spotlight may ask you to enable the feature.

These are separate features: Apple's Tahoe history lives in Spotlight, while the Clipboard History app provides its own history and shortcut on Tahoe, Sequoia, and Sonoma.

Use Apple's clipboard history in macOS Tahoe

  1. Open Spotlight. Press Command + Space.
  2. Open the Clipboard view. Press Command + 4, or select the Clipboard button beside the search field.
  3. Find the earlier item. Type in the Spotlight search field to filter your clipboard history.
  4. Copy and paste it. Select the item and press Command + C, click the destination, then press Command + V.

You can clear the built-in history from the More menu in the Clipboard view. Apple also warns that sensitive information may appear there, so review and clear it on a shared Mac.

Version check: choose Apple menu → About This Mac. If the version starts with 26, these Spotlight instructions apply. They do not apply to Sequoia 15 or Sonoma 14.

When a dedicated clipboard manager still helps

Apple's built-in history is the simplest choice when you occasionally need a recent copy. A dedicated manager makes more sense when clipboard history is part of your daily workflow and you want persistent organization or a direct global shortcut.

Clipboard History opens with Shift + Command + V from any app. It stores 1,000 items by default—adjustable from 100 to 10,000—and adds search, favorites, groups, and file or folder references.

  • Fewer steps: open the history directly instead of opening Spotlight and switching views.
  • Persistent organization: star reusable items or place them in groups so they are not removed by the normal history limit.
  • Finder workflows: keep references to copied files and folders, then paste them again or reveal the original in Finder.
  • Local control: history stays on one Mac, with no account or cloud sync.

Know the format support before you install

Clipboard History records plain text and links, preserves RTF or HTML with a plain-text fallback, and stores direct PNG or TIFF clipboard images with bounded preview thumbnails. It also supports image files and other Finder items by reference.

It does not perform OCR, QR recognition, or image-content search, and it is not an image editor or visual gallery. Read the files, folders, and image guide for examples.

It also does not sync history between Macs, iPhones, or iPads. That local-only design is useful if you prefer not to place clipboard history in a cloud service; it is a limitation if cross-device access is essential.

Treat every clipboard history as sensitive

A history can contain email addresses, private messages, access codes, and other text you did not intend to keep. Before choosing any app, check whether it ignores concealed clipboard data, lets you exclude applications, and makes deletion easy.

Clipboard History skips copies made while a supported password manager is frontmost and entries marked concealed, transient, or automatically generated.
