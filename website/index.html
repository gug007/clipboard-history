Clipboard History for Mac — never lose what you copy

Clipboard History is a free clipboard manager for macOS. It remembers the text, links, files, and folders you copy, so you can paste any of them back later. Press Shift + Command + V to open your clipboard history.

Requires macOS 14 (Sonoma) or later. Apple Silicon and Intel Macs supported. Free and open source.

What Clipboard History does

  • Brings back any text, link, or file you've recently copied — your last 1,000 copies by default, configurable from 100 up to 10,000.
  • Opens with one keyboard shortcut — Shift + Command + V by default, changeable to anything in Settings.
  • Instant search: the field is focused the moment the panel opens; it matches text, links, and filenames.
  • Files and folders copied in Finder are saved by reference — with thumbnails, click-to-preview, and Reveal in Finder — and paste back as real files.
  • Star favorites and organize clips into named groups; starred and grouped clips are never auto-removed.
  • Works completely offline. Your clipboard history never leaves your computer.
  • Automatically skips password managers (1Password, Bitwarden, Dashlane, KeePassXC, Apple Passwords, Keychain Access, LastPass) and anything any app marks as confidential on the clipboard.
  • Pause recording any time from the menu bar; it stays paused until you resume.
  • System, Light, and Dark appearance.
  • Opens on the screen you're actually using — even over fullscreen apps.
  • Lives in the menu bar with no Dock icon; starts at login (you can turn that off).
  • Free, open source, and ad-free.

Frequently asked questions

Is Clipboard History free?

Yes. Free, open source, no account, no trial, no ads. The code is on GitHub if you want to look at it.

Will it record my passwords?

No. Anything copied from 1Password, Bitwarden, Dashlane, KeePassXC, Apple Passwords, Keychain Access, or LastPass is skipped. Beyond that list, anything any app marks as concealed or transient on the clipboard — the standard convention password managers follow — is never recorded either. You can add more apps to the skip list in Settings → Privacy, or pause recording entirely from the menu bar.

Which Macs does it work on?

Any Mac running macOS 14 (Sonoma) or newer. Apple Silicon and Intel both supported.

Will it slow down my Mac?

No. The app is about 5 MB and spends its life doing one cheap thing: glancing at the clipboard twice a second. Most people forget it's running.

How is this different from the built-in macOS clipboard?

macOS only remembers the last thing you copied. Copy something new and the previous one is gone. Clipboard History keeps your last 1,000 items by default (adjustable from 100 to 10,000 in Settings) so you can paste any of them back with Shift + Command + V. Starred clips and clips you've put in a group are never auto-removed.

How does it compare to Paste, Maccy, or Alfred's clipboard?

Honestly: it's free, open source, and tiny (~5 MB). Paste is paid and syncs across devices. Maccy is also free and open source — a great alternative if you prefer it. Alfred's clipboard comes bundled with the Powerpack. Clipboard History focuses on doing one thing well, with privacy as the default. If you need iCloud sync between Macs, pick something else.

Does it sync between my Macs?

No, and it won't. Your clipboard often contains passwords, private messages, and tokens — sending that to a server (even Apple's) is a tradeoff we don't want to make on your behalf. Clipboard History stays on the Mac it's installed on.

Where is my clipboard data stored?

In a single SQLite file on your Mac at ~/Library/Application Support/Clipboard History/clipboard.sqlite. Nothing is uploaded anywhere. The file is usually a few hundred kilobytes since the app stores the location of large files, not a copy.

What about copied files and folders?

Files and folders you copy in Finder are saved by reference — the app remembers where each file lives, plus a small preview thumbnail, so a 5 GB video costs a few kilobytes. Return pastes the real file, Command + R reveals it in Finder, and clicking the thumbnail shows a quick preview. If the original gets moved or deleted, the clip is flagged so you're never surprised. One honest limitation: images copied straight to the clipboard — a screenshot captured to clipboard, or a browser's Copy Image — aren't recorded yet. Copy the image as a file and it works.

Can I change the shortcut? The theme?

Both. Record any keyboard shortcut in Settings → General — Shift + Command + V is just the default. And the app follows your choice of System, Light, or Dark appearance: switch it in Settings or with the sun/moon button right inside the overlay.

Does it start automatically?

Yes. It registers itself as a login item on first launch so your history is always being kept, and you can turn that off in Settings → General. The app lives in your menu bar — there's no Dock icon to clutter things up.

Why does it need Accessibility permission?

So it can paste for you when you press Return on a clip. macOS requires Accessibility access for any app to send keystrokes to another app. If you decline, the clip still lands on your clipboard and you can paste it manually with Command + V.

How do updates work?

The download is signed and notarized by Apple, so you won't see the scary "unidentified developer" warning. The app checks for updates in the background and every update is cryptographically verified before it installs (it uses Sparkle, the standard open-source updater for Mac apps). You can also check manually from the menu bar.

How do I uninstall Clipboard History?

Quit the app from the menu bar, then drag Clipboard History from your Applications folder to the Trash. To remove your stored history too, delete the folder at ~/Library/Application Support/Clipboard History. You can also wipe your history without uninstalling: Settings → Storage → Clear all history.
