Maccy vs Clipboard History

Both are free, open-source, focused Mac apps. Maccy is the established minimalist with flexible paste actions; Clipboard History adds named groups and a detailed file-and-folder workflow.

Evidence linkedNo affiliate linksReviewed July 28, 2026

The short answer

Choose Maccy if you want a mature, deliberately minimal utility, Homebrew or App Store installation, and a documented “paste without formatting” action. Choose Clipboard History if named groups, searchable file and folder references, previews, and an explicit local-only, no-telemetry design better match your workflow.

There is no bad choice here. Maccy describes itself as doing one job with “no fluff,” and its public source and MIT license back up the free, open-source positioning. Clipboard History is also MIT-licensed and focused on clipboard recall rather than becoming a general launcher.

When Maccy is the better fit

  • You want a proven minimalist. Maccy has a deliberately narrow interface and a long-running public project.
  • You paste plain text often. Its documented no-formatting shortcut is a practical advantage today.
  • You prefer Homebrew or the App Store. Maccy officially supports both routes in addition to direct downloads.
  • Pins are enough organization. Maccy keeps pinned items at the top and assigns direct numbered shortcuts.

When Clipboard History is the better fit

  • You copy files and folders in Finder. File references, thumbnails, Quick Look-style previews, missing-file warnings, and Reveal in Finder make that workflow explicit.
  • You want named collections. Groups separate project clips, while Favorites gives starred items a permanent home.
  • You want an explicit no-network posture for history. Clipboard data stays in a local SQLite file and does not sync.
  • You like the Shift-Command-V model. The panel opens ready to search, with direct Command-number paste shortcuts.

Clipboard History limitations to know first

Clipboard History is not the automatic winner just because this page lives on its website. It currently has several meaningful limits:

  • No Mac-to-Mac, iPhone, or iPad sync.
  • Direct PNG and TIFF clipboard images are supported with bounded previews, but there is no OCR, QR recognition, or image-content search.
  • No clipboard editor or dedicated paste-as-plain-text command.
  • macOS 14 or later only, and distribution is currently through a direct DMG rather than the App Store or Homebrew.
  • Copied files are references, not backups. Moving or deleting the original means the app cannot paste that file from its old location.

If any of those are central to your day, Maccy—or another product in the comparison guide—may serve you better.
