Alfred vs Clipboard History

Alfred adds clipboard history, snippets, merging, and automation to its paid Powerpack productivity suite. Clipboard History is a free standalone utility for people who only want searchable local history.

Evidence linkedNo affiliate linksReviewed July 27, 2026

The short answer

Choose Alfred Powerpack if you want clipboard items to feed snippets, text expansion, custom workflows, file navigation, and a deeply configurable Mac launcher. Choose Clipboard History if you only need a searchable clipboard, want named groups and Finder references, or do not want to buy a broader automation suite.

Alfred’s free launcher does not include clipboard history: the feature is part of the Powerpack. That price buys much more than a clipboard manager, which can be excellent value—or unnecessary scope—depending on how you work.

When Alfred is the better fit

  • You want automation, not just recall. Clipboard placeholders, merging, workflows, and scriptable actions can turn copied content into multi-step processes.
  • You maintain a snippet library. Auto-expansion and shareable collections are much more powerful than basic Favorites and groups.
  • You already own Powerpack. Its clipboard history is included, so a separate manager may add little.
  • You want one launcher for many jobs. File navigation, custom searches, contacts, themes, and workflows make Alfred the broader system.

When Clipboard History is the better fit

  • You only need clipboard history. Installation and preferences stay focused on one job.
  • You want to pay nothing. Clipboard capture, search, groups, favorites, and file handling have no license gate.
  • You prefer an item-count limit. Up to 10,000 items—and permanent grouped or starred items—may fit better than deleting all ordinary history after a time window.
  • You want inspectable software. The complete source and MIT license are public.

Clipboard History limitations to know first

  • No bitmap image history, while Alfred can record images.
  • No text expansion, clipboard merging, workflow variables, scripts, or automation builder.
  • No synchronization between Macs and no shared snippet collections.
  • No general launcher, file search, contacts, custom web searches, or theme editor.
  • File and folder items are references rather than backups and depend on the original remaining available.

If Alfred’s automation or snippets would replace other utilities for you, Powerpack can be the better overall value despite not being free.
