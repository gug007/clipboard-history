Raycast vs Clipboard History

Raycast bundles a sophisticated clipboard command into a broad productivity platform. Clipboard History is a smaller standalone app built around local search, groups, and Finder file references.

Evidence linkedNo affiliate linksReviewed July 27, 2026

The short answer

Choose Raycast if you already use its launcher or want image capture, on-device OCR, format-aware pasting, editing, snippets, sequential paste, and optional AI actions. Choose Clipboard History if you want a focused free app with no AI, account, cloud sync, or app telemetry—and named groups plus detailed Finder-file handling matter more than image tools.

When Raycast is clearly the better fit

  • You already live in Raycast. Adding its clipboard command avoids running a separate app and places clips beside your other commands.
  • You copy images. Clipboard History cannot currently compete with Raycast’s bitmap history, OCR, and QR extraction.
  • You transform clips before pasting. Editing, format selection, plain-text preference, snippets, and sequential paste are substantive advantages.
  • You want a wider productivity system. Extensions, Quicklinks, window management, AI tools, and other Raycast features go far beyond clipboard recall.

When Clipboard History is the better fit

  • You want one small utility. There is no launcher, extension store, AI layer, or paid feature tier to configure.
  • You organize by project. Named groups and Favorites are persistent, simple destinations rather than a larger snippet system.
  • You copy large files and folders. The app stores references and previews rather than duplicating the data, then reveals the original in Finder.
  • You require open source and local-only history. The application code is public, and clipboard records do not sync to a service.

Learn what macOS itself now provides in the Mac clipboard history guide, or compare other free choices in the clipboard-manager roundup.

Clipboard History limitations to know first

  • No direct bitmap image capture, OCR, QR-code extraction, or visual content-type filters.
  • No clip editor, paste-as-format menu, sequential paste, AI actions, or extensibility layer.
  • No device sync; history intentionally remains on one Mac.
  • macOS 14 or later only. There are no iPhone, iPad, or Windows versions.
  • Finder items are stored by reference, so they are not backups and cannot paste if the original is gone.

If you need two or more of those features, Raycast is likely the more capable choice even if Clipboard History’s simpler privacy model appeals to you.
