Paste vs Clipboard History

Paste is a polished clipboard library for Mac, iPhone, and iPad with rich content and optional iCloud sync. Clipboard History is a free, open-source Mac utility that deliberately keeps every record local.

Evidence linkedNo affiliate linksReviewed July 28, 2026

The short answer

Choose Paste if you want a visual history with images, editing, flexible filters, pinboards, and access across Apple devices. Choose Clipboard History if you want a completely free, inspectable Mac app with no subscription, account, app analytics, or cloud copy of your history.

These products optimize for different priorities. Paying for Paste buys a broader, multi-device experience. Clipboard History’s narrower feature set is the tradeoff behind its free and local-only model.

When Paste is the better fit

  • Your clipboard should follow you. Paste is designed for a library shared across Macs, iPhones, and iPads through your private iCloud account.
  • You work visually or edit clips before pasting. Paste’s gallery, editing, and original/plain-text choices remain material advantages even though both apps retain copied images and rich content.
  • You need stronger retrieval filters. Time, application, device, and content-type filters help with a very large library.
  • You share reusable material. Shared pinboards and Teams plans address collaboration that Clipboard History does not attempt.

When Clipboard History is the better fit

  • You do not want another subscription or purchase. Every current feature is free.
  • You want source-level transparency. The full Swift application is public under an MIT license.
  • Clipboard data must remain on one machine. There is no account or sync path to configure, and the app sends no usage telemetry.
  • Your workflow centers on Finder items. File and folder references, previews, missing-file states, and Reveal in Finder are core parts of the app.

Clipboard History limitations to know first

  • No iCloud sync and no iPhone or iPad app.
  • Direct PNG and TIFF images are retained, but there is no visual gallery, OCR, QR recognition, image-content search, or image editor.
  • No in-app editing, rich-format selector, multi-item stack, or shared groups.
  • No filters for source app, device, date, or content type.
  • Finder items are location references rather than backups; if an original moves or is deleted, its old reference cannot paste it.

For multi-device creative work, Paste is the stronger product. Clipboard History is intended for people who consciously prefer a smaller, local-only Mac tool.
